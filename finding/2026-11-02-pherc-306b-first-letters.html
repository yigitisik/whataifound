First text read in the Herculaneum scroll PHerc. 306B

Two Vesuvius Challenge contestants combined a public ink-detection model with one they fine-tuned themselves to recover readable Greek from PHerc. 306B, a carbonised Herculaneum scroll nobody had read, where the papyrology team agreed on the phrase "that which is in itself desirable".

What was found

PHerc. 306 was buried by the eruption of Vesuvius in AD 79, excavated in the 1750s and micro-CT scanned in 2025, and is broken into two parts. Identifying ink in the larger part, PHerc. 306B, also settled which part is which: the script orientation shows 306B is the upper section of the original roll. A surface inside the scan was grown with VC3D's GrowPatch tool from a seed on a line found by screening windings, then ink was called only where two models agreed, by multiplying the output of the public TRACE v3B model with a model the pair fine-tuned from Youssef Nader's v8-in, trained half on labels from a different scroll and half on machine labels averaged from four public models, with an added penalty for marking blank papyrus as ink. The sequence τoδιαυτo\tau o \delta \iota \alpha \upsilon \tau o had been visible in several submissions for about a month, but the following letters were read as either mu, nu or alpha-iota until this image made a diagonal middle stroke visible and the papyrology team agreed on alpha-iota, giving a reading with parallels in Aristotle, Sextus Empiricus and Philodemus' On Anger. Twenty-five submissions were reviewed on this scroll and several others reached 7 to 9 characters against a 10-character target.

Novelty check

This registry already carries 2026-06-25-herculaneum-scroll-read, which is a different scroll, PHerc. 1667, read end to end. PHerc. 306B had no entry, and the announcement states no one had read what was written in it since its excavation. The prize criterion itself is the novelty claim being made, 10 readable characters in 4 square centimetres of a scroll with no prior reading, and the post records the competing submissions that fell short on the same segment. No author or work has been attributed to the scroll yet, only textual parallels.

Caveats and known objections

Author verified rather than independent: the reading was agreed by the Vesuvius Challenge's own papyrology team, which is part of the challenge, and no outside papyrologist has published on it. The result is a few words, not a text, and the readings are probabilistic reconstructions from a learned ink signal where ink is called only where two models agree, which raises precision at the cost of recall. The $50,000 First Letters Prize marks the milestone and is not an independent check. Autonomy is ai-assisted on the same reading as the earlier scroll entry: the models produce ink probabilities and a human papyrologist resolved the letters, and it was a human eye on a newly visible stroke that settled the ambiguous reading. Code, model weights and data are public, so the detection step is reproducible.
