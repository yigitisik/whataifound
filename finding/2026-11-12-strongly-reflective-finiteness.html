Finiteness of strongly reflective modular forms, answering Borcherds and Gritsenko–Nikulin, with AI used in parts of the proof

Two Wuhan University mathematicians prove that up to conjugation and scaling there are only finitely many strongly reflective modular forms on orthogonal groups of signature (n, 2) with n at least 3, settling a 1998 conjecture of Gritsenko and Nikulin and answering a question Borcherds posed in 1995; they say they used generative AI interactively to develop, test and refine parts of the proof after building the framework themselves.

What was found

A modular form on an orthogonal arithmetic group is strongly reflective if its zero divisor is a reduced union of mirrors of reflections in the group. Such forms, often Borcherds products, are tied to reflective lattices, hyperbolic reflection groups and generalized Kac-Moody algebras. The paper proves finiteness up to conjugation and scaling, and more generally for reflective forms whose mirror multiplicities are bounded by any fixed m (its Theorem 1.3); as consequences it gets the finiteness of holomorphic automorphic products of singular weight and of the groups whose algebra of modular forms is freely generated. The key ingredient is a uniform upper bound on the covolume of the group, obtained by adapting the higher-rank lattice convergence theorem of Abért and coauthors, on invariant random subgroups, to the degrees of reflective branch divisors.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-23: the preprint is 2611.16819 (math.NT), dated 12 November 2026; it presents the result as answering Borcherds's 1995 question and settling the Gritsenko-Nikulin conjecture of 1998, which earlier work had proved only in special cases. Caveats and known objections

Capped at claimed: an unrefereed preprint with no formalization and no outside check yet. The watch report that surfaced it flagged the title as an overclaim, but the paper's abstract does claim the full conjecture, for signature (n, 2) with n at least 3, so the claim is as stated. Autonomy is ai-assisted: the disclosure says the authors developed the initial framework and proof ideas and then used generative AI interactively for literature searches and for developing, testing and refining parts of the proof, which keeps the research direction human. The model is not named.
