First lower bounds above n for Naslund's crossing problem, found and proved in Lean by an agent team

A multi-agent harness of Claude models run by Arth Singh proved in Lean, over every field, that the quantity P(n) in Naslund's crossing problem is at least 4n/3, then 15n/11 and 67n/49 for larger n, and that P(4) = 7, the first lower bounds above the trivial n, though all are linear and leave Naslund's superlinear question open.

What was found

P(n)P(n) is the least r+sr + s such that the tensor [x1=x4][x2=x3][x_1 = x_4][x_2 = x_3] on [n]4[n]^4 is a sum of rr tensors of rank one along one pairing of the coordinates and ss along another; equivalently, the least rank⁡B+rank⁡R(C)\operatorname{rank} B + \operatorname{rank} R(C) over splittings of the swap matrix as B+CB + C, with RR the realignment. Naslund asked, as Problem 12 of his paper (arXiv 2017, JCTA 2020), whether P(n)≥n1+εP(n) \ge n^{1+\varepsilon}. The repository proves P(n)≥4n/3P(n) \ge 4n/3 for n≥3n \ge 3, 15n/1115n/11 for n≥5n \ge 5, 67n/4967n/49 for n≥7n \ge 7, n+3n + 3 for n≥4n \ge 4, P(5)≥8P(5) \ge 8, P(6)≥9P(6) \ge 9 and P(4)=7P(4) = 7, with an exact-arithmetic script showing the border value at n=4n = 4 is lower. The paper also shows that no argument of the slice kind used here can reach 2n2n. The README's account of the run says one agent posed the lower-bound question and proved the trivial bound unprompted, a second improved it, the operator then set the goal of going further, and the bounds above followed within about 74 minutes.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-16, abs:Naslund returned 17 records, none with a lower bound on this quantity; the nearest are on partition rank and slice rank. The repository reports searching Naslund's paper, Karam (2310.11356), Hrubes, Wigderson and Yehudayoff (2011), Hrubes (2024), Brand, Kaski and Wang (2607.02241) and an appendix of Hexagon 2610.00577 without finding a bound above nn, and says this is a search result rather than a proof of novelty. Nothing in this registry covered the problem.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal on the Palomar registration, trust level high, for the seven statements exactly as written in an 88-line Challenge.lean that imports one Mathlib module. Not formalized: the translation between Naslund's tensor form and the matrix form the Lean states, the upper bounds, and the remarks. Only the 4n/3 bound has been checked by hand, by the author's side and in one review of a draft; the others are Lean-only and, the README says, no person has read them in full. The Lean files are concatenated agent output running to thousands of lines, still carrying the agents' own names for the definitions with a bridge to the stated ones. The paper is unrefereed and the result is a day old. Autonomy is ai-led: the agents posed the question and found the first bounds before any person asked, but the operator then set the goals and directed the harness, which humans built.
