Deep learning on routine chest radiographs predicts major osteoporotic fractures better than FRAX

A convolutional survival model trained on 53,109 adults at a Korean tertiary hospital and validated on 85,284 patients from two other institutions predicted incident major osteoporotic fracture from routine chest radiographs with a C-index of 0.843, against 0.779 for FRAX with bone density.

Original work

DOINEJM AI: Opportunistic Risk Assessment of Major Osteoporotic Fractures from Routine Chest Radiographs Using Deep Learning↗

What was found

Bone density and FRAX miss much of the fracture risk in the osteopenic range, where most fractures occur. Participants needed a chest radiograph within a year of a DXA scan. In the external cohorts (mean age 58.6, 68.9% female, 77 months of follow-up) the model reached a C-index of 0.843 (95% CI 0.837 to 0.850) and a censoring-adjusted 10-year AUROC of 0.855, against 0.779 and 0.785 for FRAX with BMD (both P<0.01P < 0.01); adding FRAX to the model barely changed it (C-index 0.847). In the osteopenia subgroup of 35,358 patients the 10-year AUROC was 0.779 against 0.659, and the advantage held across fracture types and BMD categories. The study was funded by the National Research Foundation of Korea and others.

Novelty check

Europe PMC, 2026-11-19: the DOI returns the NEJM AI record, published 16 November 2026. Opportunistic osteoporosis screening from chest radiographs and CT with deep learning is an established line, so the new element is a survival model for incident fracture compared head to head with FRAX on external cohorts, not the idea of reading bone health from chest films. Nothing in this registry covers osteoporosis or fracture prediction.

Caveats and known objections

Retrospective. Every participant had been referred for densitometry, so the cohort is not the unselected routine-radiography population the method is meant for, and the authors themselves say absolute-risk and case-finding performance needs prospective evaluation there. The abstract calls the two validation institutions independent but does not name them; the author list spans Seoul National University Hospital, Seoul National University Bundang Hospital and Chungnam National University, so the validation is not treated as one by unaffiliated investigators in this registry's sense. The outcome, incident fracture, is a hard endpoint, but discrimination is not the same as showing that acting on the score prevents fractures. Model weights and data access were not checked. Autonomy is ai-assisted: the model produces a risk score that clinicians would act on.
