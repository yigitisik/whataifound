Proof of Euler's 1747 conjecture on sums of two integers with prime factors 1 mod 4, drafted by OpenAI Codex

Four Purdue researchers prove that every positive integer n≡2(mod4)n \equiv 2 \pmod 4 is a sum of two positive integers whose prime factors are all ≡1(mod4)\equiv 1 \pmod 4, with 1 allowed, as Euler proposed to Goldbach in 1747, and say that OpenAI Codex generated the argument, performed the exact calculations and drafted the manuscript under their direction.

What was found

Euler stated the assertion in a letter to Goldbach of 6 May 1747. Dietmann and Elsholtz proved in 2019 that it holds for every sufficiently large nn, without an explicit threshold. The new proof counts all representations of nn by four squares and bounds those in which a prime p≡3(mod4)p \equiv 3 \pmod 4 divides both coordinates of either pair; an average over a nonsplit torus normalizer turns the quaternionic Hecke bound, available through Jacquet-Langlands and Deligne, into an error independent of the sieving prime, and elementary estimates then give the explicit threshold 644=16,777,21664^4 = 16{,}777{,}216. An exact arithmetic certificate covers the 4,194,3044{,}194{,}304 integers in the residue class below it, by a sieve whose counts the paper tabulates.

Novelty check

Caveats and known objections

Graded claimed: the finite part is checkable but the analytic part, which carries every nn above 16,777,21616{,}777{,}216, has not been checked by anyone outside the authors, and it rests on deep inputs (the Hecke bound through Jacquet-Langlands and Deligne) whose constants have to be explicit for the threshold to hold. For this entry the registry recomputed the finite certificate independently, sieving the 4,194,3044{,}194{,}304 integers below the threshold, and reproduced every count in the paper's table, with all of them covered; that confirms the computation, not the analytic estimate. Hexagon labels the text primarily AI-generated and records the authors' understanding as covering some parts. Autonomy is ai-led: the paper says Codex generated the mathematical argument, did the exact calculations and drafted the manuscript under human direction, so the key insight is the AI's, while the humans chose the problem, directed the work and stand behind it.

Independent checks

whataifound (this registry): An independent sieve over the integers n≡2(mod4)n \equiv 2 \pmod 4 below 64464^4 reproduced the paper's finite certificate: 1,344,661 retained indices and all twelve tabulated counts, ending at zero, with every integer covered and a brute-force sample agreeing. The analytic estimate above the threshold was not checked. · link ↗
