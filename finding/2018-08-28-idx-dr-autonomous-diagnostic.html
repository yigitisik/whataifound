Pivotal trial of an autonomous AI diagnostic for diabetic retinopathy (IDx-DR)

In a prospective trial at 10 US primary care offices, an AI system that grades retinal photographs with no clinician reading them detected more-than-mild diabetic retinopathy with 87.2% sensitivity and 90.7% specificity against a specialist reading-centre reference, exceeding every prespecified endpoint; the results were the basis for the first FDA authorization of an autonomous AI diagnostic.

Original work

Naturenpj Digital Medicine: Pivotal trial of an autonomous AI-based diagnostic system for detection of diabetic retinopathy in primary care offices↗

What was found

900 people with diabetes and no known retinopathy were enrolled, of whom 819 could be fully analysed. Existing primary care staff, after standardised training, took the photographs with instant image-quality feedback from the system, which then returned a disease-level result, positive or negative for more-than-mild retinopathy or diabetic macular oedema, with no clinician interpreting the images. The reference standard was widefield stereoscopic photography and macular OCT graded by the Wisconsin Fundus Photograph Reading Center. Prespecified superiority endpoints were sensitivity above 85% and specificity above 82.5%; imageability was 96.1%. The FDA granted De Novo authorization in April 2018, before the paper appeared. Later versions were cleared by 510(k), including IDx-DR v2.3 in 2022.

Novelty check

Europe PMC, titles (artificial intelligence OR deep learning OR autonomous) AND diabetic retinopathy AND (prospective OR pivotal, or clinical-trial publication type), before 28 August 2018: no hits. Retrospective deep-learning grading of retinal photographs had been published (Gulshan et al., JAMA, 2016). What was new was a prospective, preregistered trial in primary care with the AI as the only reader, and the authorization that followed.

Caveats and known objections

Funded by IDx, whose founder, Michael Abràmoff, led the study; the reference grading was done by an independent reading centre. This entry found no published real-world accuracy study of the system by an unaffiliated group. Sensitivity of 87% means roughly one referable case in eight was missed against the reference standard, at the 24% prevalence of this trial population. Autonomy is graded autonomous for the diagnostic read only: who was screened, and what happened after a referral, followed a pathway clinicians designed.
