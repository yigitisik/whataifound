A shortened CAR intracellular domain designed with Claude Code

Six scientists at Generate Biomedicines used Claude Code to propose new designs for the intracellular signalling region of a CAR T-cell receptor; one design was about 70 percent shorter than the standard version and, in cell assays, let immune cells display roughly 10 times as many receptors, kill tumour cells more efficiently and stay active longer, with no animal or patient data reported.

What was found

CAR T-cell therapy has mostly treated cancers of the blood cells, and Generate Biomedicines is trying to reengineer it to treat solid tumours as well. Its approach is to shorten one part of the therapy's genetic instructions so that there is room for further instructions suited to other kinds of cancer. Six Generate scientists used Claude Code to propose new designs for that part, and one proposal was about 70 percent shorter than the standard version. In laboratory tests that design beat the standard on three measures at once: immune cells made roughly 10 times as many receptors, the proteins they use to spot cancer cells, they killed tumour cells more efficiently, and they stayed active for longer. The announcement reports no animal work and no clinical work, and gives no affinities, replicate counts or statistics.

Novelty check

Searched Europe PMC on 2026-10-27. Compacting the intracellular signalling region of a chimeric antigen receptor is an established and crowded strategy rather than a new idea: CAR AND "intracellular domain" AND (truncated OR shortened OR refactored) AND "solid tumor" returned 490 hits, and "chimeric antigen receptor" AND (minimal OR compact OR truncated) AND "signaling domain" AND expression returned 2,320. What the announcement presents as new is one specific design and its measured effect, not the strategy of shortening the domain, and this entry is graded on that basis. No existing registry entry covers CAR T-cell engineering; the nearest Anthropic biology entries are 2026-10-20-claude-scorpion-toxin-binders, on de novo binders, and 2026-09-23-array-associated-reverse-transcriptases, on phage genomics, and neither overlaps. Generate's technical report was not read for this entry, so no search was run against Generate's own earlier publications on the same construct.

Caveats and known objections

Entered at claimed under the rule that lab announcements start there. Generate Biomedicines ran the work and Anthropic is its partner in it, so both organisations named have a stake in the result and no one outside the collaboration has assessed it. The evidence is cell-assay only: receptor count, efficiency of tumour-cell killing and duration of activity in culture, all surrogate measures rather than any patient outcome, and the announcement reports no animal study and no human study. Every figure is approximate in the source itself, about 70 percent shorter and roughly 10 times as many receptors, and the announcement gives no replicate counts, no statistics, no comparator beyond the standard version of the domain, and no identification of the target antigen or the solid tumour type. Generate's technical report is linked and was not read for this entry, so the counts and the assay design above come only from Anthropic's post. Autonomy is graded ai-assisted on the strictest defensible reading: six human scientists drove the project, set the goal of shortening the domain and ran all the testing, with Claude Code proposing designs inside that frame. A reading that treated the winning design as the key insight would reach ai-led and is defensible; the weaker grade is taken because the announcement describes the scientists as the ones using Claude Code rather than the model as running the campaign. Field is biology rather than medicine because no therapeutic or clinical endpoint was reached. Discussion of the announcement questioned how much of the work across the whole post was scientific rather than clerical, and characterised this case as improving an existing therapeutic rather than originating one.
