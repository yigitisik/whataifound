Randomised trial of AI-ECG guided monitoring for undetected atrial fibrillation

In a single-centre trial in Taiwan, 14,665 adults without known atrial fibrillation were randomised to have their past year of sinus-rhythm ECGs analysed by an AI model, with the 1.1% it called high risk offered up to seven days of Holter monitoring, and the composite of newly detected atrial fibrillation, flutter or pre-atrial-fibrillation findings at 90 days was 0.74% against 0.48%, a result the authors describe as statistically fragile.

What was found

Atrial fibrillation often goes undetected until a stroke, and the attraction of an AI-ECG model is that it claims to see a signature of atrial vulnerability on an ordinary ECG recorded in sinus rhythm, when nothing is visible to a reader. Adults aged 40 to 85 with no previous atrial fibrillation or flutter were randomised to an AI-ECG-guided strategy or usual care, 7,337 against 7,328. Within the intervention arm, participants whose ECGs from the preceding year were scored high risk were offered Holter monitoring; 84 participants (1.1%) were flagged and 48 of them (57.1%) were monitored. The design deals with the obvious bias that monitoring finds what monitoring looks for: each monitored participant was paired with an age- and sex-matched control chosen before the control arm's ECGs were analysed, and those controls received identical monitoring, with reporting cardiologists blinded to the AI results and to allocation. The primary composite occurred in 54 intervention participants against 35 controls, an odds ratio of 1.54 (95% CI 1.01 to 2.37, P=0.046P = 0.046), and the effect was concentrated as designed, at 32.1% against 2.6% among high-risk participants and 0.4% against 0.5% among the rest.

Caveats and known objections

Single-centre and open-label, with the model's developers running the trial, so no independent validation. The result is fragile on the authors' own account: reclassifying a single event as a non-event moves the primary PP value from 0.046 to 0.057. The composite endpoint carries most of the signal in pre-atrial-fibrillation findings, frequent or short runs of premature atrial complexes and non-sustained episodes, whose clinical meaning is unsettled; detection of actual atrial fibrillation or flutter was 0.3% against 0.2% and not significantly different, and initiation of antiarrhythmic therapy at 180 days was 2.3% against 2.4%, essentially identical. No stroke, mortality or anticoagulation outcome was measured, and at 90 days none could be. Only 57.1% of flagged high-risk participants actually underwent monitoring, so the pathway as delivered reached just over half the people it identified. Monitoring itself caused participant-reported harms in 22.9% of monitored intervention participants against 18.8% of matched controls, mostly skin reactions and discomfort. The ClinicalTrials.gov record still lists the trial as recruiting with an estimated completion of 2026-10-01 and an estimated enrolment of 14,726, against the 14,665 randomised in the published report, so the registration has not been updated to match. Autonomy is ai-assisted: the model selected who was offered monitoring, and blinded cardiologists read every recording and made the diagnosis.
