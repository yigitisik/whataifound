Exact diameter of the symmetric group with LRX generators, n(n−1)/2 for every n ≥ 4

Seven authors report that their agentic system, mainly GPT-6 Astra, proved the undirected Cayley graph of SnS_n generated by the cyclic shift, its inverse and the transposition of the first two positions has diameter n(n−1)/2n(n-1)/2 for every n≥4n \ge 4, with a finite set of special cases checked by C++ code and the result registered on Palomar as a Lean proof.

What was found

With LL the cyclic shift, R=L−1R = L^{-1} and XX the transposition of the first two positions, the question is the largest distance between two permutations in the Cayley graph, OEIS A186783; the paper says the conjectured value was open since at least 2011, and the CayleyPy growth computations listed it among their conjectures. The paper establishes sharp upper and lower bounds by combinatorial and group-theoretic arguments, leaving a finite set of special cases verified by accompanying C++ code. The Palomar record proves the upper and lower bounds and constructs an explicit L, R, X word of at most that length between every ordered pair of permutations, against a Mathlib-only Challenge.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-19, abs:"symmetric group" AND abs:"Cayley graph" AND abs:diameter AND abs:transposition returned 5 records, including a 2020 upper bound for a related generator set and the CayleyPy papers that list this diameter as a conjecture; none proves it. Nothing in this registry covers Cayley graph diameters.

Caveats and known objections

Held at claimed on a conservative reading. Palomar records trust level high and a nanoda kernel check of PalomarLRX.exactDiameter and PalomarLRX.boundedRouting at a pinned commit, which for other entries in this registry has supported formal; the registration is by a third-party repository, snow-ghost/lrx-lean, whose relationship to the authors is not stated, and no human outside the group has audited its Challenge statement. The finite cases in the paper rest on C++ code, not on the Lean development, as far as the paper says. Hexagon labels the text a mix of human-written and AI-generated with human understanding of some parts: Section 1 is mostly human-written and the rest, including the proof, AI-generated with minor human edits. Autonomy is ai-led rather than autonomous: the authors describe human intervention as relatively minor and mostly workflow orchestration, and the problem was posed by humans.
