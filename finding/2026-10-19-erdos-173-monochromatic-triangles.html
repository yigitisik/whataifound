Erdős Problem 173: a two-coloring of the plane misses at most one triangle, which is equilateral

A Lean-checked proof that in every two-coloring of the plane, every triangle except possibly one equilateral triangle has a monochromatic congruent copy, settling a 1973 conjecture of Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and Straus, found by GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 working in alternation.

What was found

Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and Straus conjectured in 1973 that every non-equilateral triangle has a monochromatic copy in every two-coloring of the plane, and gave the alternating-strip coloring that shows one equilateral triangle can be missed. Special cases were settled for right triangles by Shader in 1976, for colorings with closed or open classes by Jelínek, Kynčl, Stolař and Valla in 2009, and for further families by Currier, Moore and Yip in 2024. By the eight-point configuration of the 1975 sequel, it suffices to show that no coloring avoids the equilateral triangles of two different side lengths. The proof does this with a spectral argument for side ratios far from one, an irrational-rotation argument on circles for ratios near one, and a transfer from arbitrary to measurable colorings adapted from OpenAI's unrefereed preprint 'The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable', extended here from the algebraic numbers to the algebraic closure of ℚ(x)\mathbb{Q}(x) for every real xx. Comparator checks five theorems, three of them, erdos_173 included, copied verbatim from Formal Conjectures.

Novelty check

On 2026-10-20 erdosproblems.com still listed Problem 173 as open with no proof claims, and Formal Conjectures carries it as open. The repository records its own search of 17 October, covering Bloom's entry and thread, the Formal Conjectures issue and pull request, arXiv abstracts on Euclidean Ramsey theory and the openai/math release, which found no proof beyond the special families above; a web search here on 2026-10-20 found nothing further. Erdős and Graham's 1979 statement, which Bloom cites, asks for more, that a single color class contain every triangle but one equilateral, and that stronger form remains open.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal on the Palomar registration, which replays the proof against Challenge.lean under the three standard axioms with two independent kernels, and on the statement: erdos_173, HasMonochromaticCopy and the Shader and strip-coloring variants are identical, apart from category attributes, to Formal Conjectures' file at commit 7db2ea3b91, written independently of the prover, and the two files' notation for the plane resolves to the same Mathlib type. The two statements that carry the non-equilateral and collinear forms are the authors' own, short and in Mathlib terms; they read as Conjecture 3 states it, but nobody without a stake has confirmed that. The grade certifies the theorem, not the prose: the blueprint was written by Claude Opus 5.5 from Plasma AI's write-up, the only review of the argument was by GPT-6 Astra and did not cover the blueprint or the Lean, and no mathematician is on record as having read it. About two-thirds of the Lean is vendored from openai/math, written by an internal OpenAI model; it builds as part of the project, so the formal grade covers it, but the mathematics it encodes comes from an unrefereed preprint. Autonomy is ai-led rather than autonomous because Plasma AI chose the problem, assembled the research brief that carried the starting route, and directed the formalization.
