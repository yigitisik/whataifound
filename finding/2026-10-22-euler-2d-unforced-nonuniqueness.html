Non-uniqueness of unforced two-dimensional Euler flows with unbounded integrable vorticity

A two-part manuscript by Matei P. Coiculescu, whose detailed proofs, code and text were largely produced with Claude Opus 5.5 under his direction, claims that the incompressible Euler equations in the plane, with no external force, admit two distinct renormalized weak solutions from the same radial initial vorticity, which lies in L1∩LqL^1 \cap L^q for every q<∞q < \infty but is unbounded; Part I is on arXiv and the whole proof is formalized in Lean.

What was found

One solution is the stationary radial vorticity itself. The other is 3-fold rotationally symmetric, equimeasurable with the datum at every time, and leaves it in finite time; the two differ in Lt∞(L1∩Lq)xL^\infty_t (L^1 \cap L^q)_x for every q∈(4/3,∞)q \in (4/3, \infty) and every large enough time horizon. Part I proves two ingredients: a compactness criterion, under which radial data approximated by 3-fold symmetric solutions whose non-radial moment stays bounded below yield a second solution, and a linear analysis showing an unstable eigenvalue for the linearization around the basic building block plus a small point vortex. Part II, the nonlinear construction, is in the repository but not on arXiv. The repository's Lean 4 development, about 1,280 files and 170,000 lines, proves that a three-part Hypothesis (N) implies the main theorem, proves (N3) directly, and proves sound a checker for the computer-assisted parts (N1) and (N2). Those were certified twice: by a Python program in rigorous ball arithmetic (FLINT/Arb), and by a compiled run of the Lean checker on 2026-10-22 that took 8.75 hours and printed true on all 261 cells.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-05: abs:"Euler" AND abs:"non-uniqueness" AND abs:"vorticity" returned 10 records, among them Vishik's forced non-uniqueness (1805.09426), an exposition of it (2112.04943) and forced vanishing-viscosity non-uniqueness (2507.19257); none is unforced. The closest result is concurrent: Palomar record PALOMAR-2026-10-28-000008, registered 2026-10-28 by Tao Tao and Haiqi Zhang, six days after this paper's arXiv version 1, formalizes non-uniqueness for the unforced Euler equations on the two-dimensional torus with vorticity in LpL^p for each finite pp, which its abstract identifies as the two-dimensional finite-LpL^p question in the first part of Buckmaster and Vicol's Problem 6. The setting differs (the torus against the plane, a different construction) and the two appear independent; that development declares human authors working with ChatGPT and an Aristotle formalization, and is not in this registry. Its sources also cite Bruè, Colombo and Kumar (arXiv:2408.07934) and Mengual (2024) on non-uniqueness with integrable or LpL^p vorticity, whose exact relation to this result was not assessed here. Within this registry 2026-09-08-euler-unforced-blowup is a finite-time blowup claim, a different question, and 2026-09-15-ipm-forced-blowup is earlier work by the same author.

Caveats and known objections

Entered at claimed. The Lean development is the author's own: it is not registered on Palomar, its build was not rerun for this entry, and no mathematician outside the project is on record as having read either part. Only Part I is on arXiv, Part II being a working draft in the repository, and neither has been refereed. The formal guarantee has a stated gap: the kernel checks that the checker is sound and that the theorem follows if the checker returns true on the certificate, but true itself comes from executing compiled code, whose trusted base is the Lean compiler, its runtime and GMP, the same base native_decide would use; the Python/Arb certificate is a second implementation, not a kernel check. The Lean sources were written against an earlier single-volume manuscript, and 39 of the 213 manuscript labels they cite have no counterpart in Parts I and II, which affects cross-referencing rather than the formal statement. Some Lean files are adapted from OpenAI's NavierStokesAndEuler repository. The AI disclosure is thin on arXiv, whose comment field says only LLM-assisted, and full in the repository README, which names Claude Opus 5.5. Autonomy is ai-assisted, the weaker of the defensible readings: the README credits the main idea, the use of non-monotone unstable radial vortices and the overall strategy to the author, and the detailed proofs, estimates, code and text largely to the model under his direction, and he states that he checked all proofs by hand.
