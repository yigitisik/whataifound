36 manuscripts across 18 fields from the BootLoops harness run with Claude

Matthew Schwartz's open-source BootLoops harness, operated by Claude, produced 36 manuscripts with 19 coauthors between July and September 2026, mostly exact or certified computations carried from physics and computer science into fields such as population genetics, phylogenetics, ecology and health policy; eight are still in preparation and none is yet listed as published in a journal.

What was found

BootLoops pools programs from particle physics, experimental mathematics, computer algebra and numerical analysis in one toolkit that a language-model agent can operate and extend. The overview paper (arXiv:2610.02286) says the computations were run by Claude with a human setting the targets and the standards, and that the package itself was written by AI apart from forks of public codes. The essay on Anthropic's blog counts 36 manuscripts in 18 fields with 19 coauthors over three months, chosen from about 400 candidate problems; the project's manuscript index lists the overview and 35 papers in nine groups, eight marked in preparation, and says every manuscript has been read by humans for accuracy, that many were drafted first by language models, and that those not yet fully checked are watermarked preliminary. The recurring move is to replace an estimate by an exact or certified answer: Feynman integrals in closed form by bootstrap, Bayesian evidence for four- and five-species phylogenetic trees as exact rational numbers, the site-frequency spectrum of a selected allele at any selection strength, Medicare star-rating cut points at the true optimum of their clustering criterion, and exact operating characteristics of published adaptive trial designs. Coauthors include Scott Edwards and Paul Lewis on phylogenetics, Michael Desai on population genetics, James O'Dwyer on ecology and Isaiah Andrews on economics. This registry's 2026-10-01-anisotropic-watson-integral is one result from the project.

Caveats and known objections

A container for the release, graded claimed: the essay and the index are the authors' own account, nothing is refereed, eight manuscripts are not public, and the index itself says some drafts have not been fully checked by humans. The strength of the work varies by kind, which is why each manuscript needs separate grading: exact rational evidences, interval-certified integrals and proven enumerations can be rerun by anyone with the published code, while the ecology fits and policy reanalyses rest on modelling choices. The overview names the failure mode its methods guard against, a bug in a computer-assisted search that silently skips part of the space, and uses planted synthetic targets as controls. Autonomy is graded ai-assisted, the weakest reading that fits the whole release: the overview says Claude ran the computations and wrote the package with a human setting targets and standards, while the essay warns that the automated checks still cannot be trusted and that Claude's conclusions can be wrong, and says the human supplies the taste in choosing problems; how much each manuscript owes to the model is not documented per paper. Schwartz is a visiting researcher at Anthropic and the essay ran on Anthropic's blog, but it states that BootLoops is not an Anthropic project and is owned and maintained by Schwartz. Which model versions produced which results is not recorded.
