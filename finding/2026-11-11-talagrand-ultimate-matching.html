Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture proved for finite critical exponents and refuted at the endpoints

Three probabilists posted a 70-page proof, largely generated and proof-checked by language models, that random points in the unit square can be matched with simultaneous exponential tail bounds in both coordinates for every pair of finite critical exponents, settling Talagrand's Ultimate Matching Conjecture in that range, and showing that it fails at the two endpoint cases.

What was found

Talagrand posed the conjecture as Research Problem 17.1.2 of his 2021 monograph: match NN independent uniform points in the unit square to evenly spread points by a single permutation whose coordinate displacements obey exponential tail bounds of exponents α1\alpha_1 and α2\alpha_2 at once, at the scale log⁡N/N\sqrt{\log N/N}, whenever 1/α1+1/α2=1/21/\alpha_1 + 1/\alpha_2 = 1/2. The paper proves this for all finite α1,α2>2\alpha_1, \alpha_2 > 2, for an evenly spread deterministic target and for a second independent sample, by building on the flat torus a current whose divergence is Gaussian white noise projected on a dyadic grid and whose coordinates satisfy the critical anisotropic Orlicz bounds, then transferring it to a matching through an optimal-transport reduction. At the endpoints (2,∞)(2,\infty) and (∞,2)(\infty,2) a Brownian local-time construction shows that the normalised optimal cost diverges, so the conjecture is false there. Exact endpoint asymptotics are claimed only in a separate file in the authors' repository.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-16, abs:Talagrand AND abs:matching AND abs:"exponential" returned one record, on vector balancing, which is unrelated. The paper builds on the authors' own 2026 work on exact asymptotics for the two-dimensional matching problem (2609.08717). This registry carries other Talagrand problems, the convolution conjecture and the operator cotype problem, but nothing on matching.

Caveats and known objections

Entered at claimed rather than author verified, on the authors' own account: their use-of-AI section says the manuscript was mostly generated and proof-checked by LLMs while they worked to understand earlier monolithic versions, that they have an overall understanding of the proofs, and that they plan to provide a fully digested version shortly. Hexagon labels the text primarily AI-generated with human understanding of some parts. Seventy unrefereed pages with no formalization and no outside reader. vibemathed's metadata for the result is internally inconsistent, labelling its verification unreviewed while showing curator approval, so it adds nothing as a check. Autonomy is collaborative: the authors describe weeks of interaction starting from their own earlier project, after which a solution of the critical case appeared, somewhat unexpectedly to them, in a later round, and GPT-6 then obtained the endpoint asymptotics. That could support ai-led, and the weaker grade wins when the reading is arguable.
