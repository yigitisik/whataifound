Matrix multiplication exponent bounded by 9/4

An OpenAI manuscript proves that two n×nn \times n complex matrices can be multiplied with O(n9/4+ε)O(n^{9/4+\varepsilon}) arithmetic operations, lowering the best bound on the matrix multiplication exponent from 2.371177 to 2.25, with a Lean proof that two outside researchers rebuilt and extended to every field within two days.

What was found

Every improvement since Coppersmith and Winograd's 2.376 of 1990 has come from refining the laser method and has moved the third or fourth decimal place; this proof does not use that route. It works in Strassen's asymptotic spectrum, with tensor characters that add on direct sums, multiply on tensor products and are monotone under restriction. A Fourier-projection separation turns blocks that share variables on one leg into independent blocks, and applied to polynomial multiplication it gives a profile P(a,b)P(a, b) with P(a,3h+a−1)≥3P(a,h)P(a, 3h + a - 1) \ge 3P(a, h) and P(1,b)=bP(1, b) = b, which force P(a,a)≥a4/3P(a, a) \ge a^{4/3}; comparing with the interpolation bound gives t≤3/4t \le 3/4 for every character and hence ω≤9/4\omega \le 9/4. Two companions in the family give ω<2.258\omega < 2.258 and α>0.465\alpha > 0.465 in characteristic zero, and ω<2.371054886006746\omega < 2.371054886006746 over every field. The result concerns the arithmetic exponent and says nothing about practical matrix sizes.

Novelty check

Read the manuscript's introduction and the Lean scope document at commit fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb on 2026-10-12. The previous published bound is 2.371177 from Dupont and colleagues in August 2026 (2026-08-17-matmul-exponent), which the manuscript cites. A separate 2.3710449 bound over every commutative ring, LeanMMBound, was pushed to its repository hours before this release (2026-10-07-matmul-exponent-2371045). No earlier claim near 2.25 was found.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal because the Lean proof has been rebuilt and extended outside OpenAI, not on OpenAI's own catalogue, which records its review status as unchecked. Jeroen Zuiddam, who works on the asymptotic spectrum of tensors the proof relies on, published a derived repository on 2026-10-07 importing 143 of OpenAI's modules (99 unchanged) and proving the bound over every field, restating the arithmetic model and proving the infimum is not vacuous; its CI build passed and its README reports a Comparator replay with only the three standard axioms. Sela Navot's separate extension, written with GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol under Navot's direction, is registered on Palomar for every field and, since 2026-10-11, every ring; each implies the complex statement. Those checks certify the Lean statement, not the manuscript: the arithmetic model is one standard definition of the exponent, and nobody outside OpenAI is on record as having read the paper's argument. Scientific American, which interviewed Virginia Vassilevska Williams, whose group set several of the previous records, on the problem's importance, still described the bound as an unverified claim on 2026-10-08. Autonomy is graded ai-led for the same reasons as the other families split from this release.

Independent checks

Jeroen Zuiddam (University of Amsterdam): Rebuilt the 143 modules of OpenAI's Lean library that the bound imports, modified 41 to remove the one use of a primitive root of unity unavailable in positive characteristic, and proved ω(F)≤9/4\omega(F) \le 9/4 for every field FF, with the arithmetic model kept verbatim from OpenAI's Comparator challenge. CI build passed on 2026-10-07; axioms reported as propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. · link ↗

Sela Navot: Independent fork extending the same proof to every field, then to every ring in a second repository; both registered on Palomar after its preflight passed. The extension was found and written by GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol under Navot's direction, so it is a check outside OpenAI but not a check without AI. · link ↗
