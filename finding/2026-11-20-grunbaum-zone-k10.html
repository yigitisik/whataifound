Lean-checked rhombic parallelohedron with ten faces in every zone, answering Grünbaum's question at k = 10

DICTO found a nonconvex polyhedron of 30 congruent golden rhombi, the rhombic triacontahedron less four oblate golden rhombohedra, in which every zone has exactly ten faces and whose lattice translates tile space face to face, and Claude Code wrote a Lean proof of these properties, giving an example for k = 10 of the question Grünbaum posed in 2010.

What was found

In the Mathematical Intelligencer in 2010, Grünbaum asked, for each even k≥4k \ge 4, whether some rhombic monohedral parallelohedron has exactly kk faces in every zone, and wrote that no information was available for any k≥10k \ge 10. The witness, which DICTO calls the Grünbaum-Escher triacontahedron GET B, is a union of 16 golden rhombohedra with 30 faces, 32 vertices and 60 edges. The Lean proof checks exact data in ℤ[φ]\mathbb{Z}[\varphi] by kernel evaluation (zones, congruence of the faces, separating normals, contacts, face matching and cone separation from an interior point) and derives the tiling from the equality of volume and covolume using Mathlib's fundamental domains. Two calibration theorems check that the zone definition gives 4 for the cube and 6 for the rhombic dodecahedron. Since GET B is an indentation of the triacontahedron, it is also a counterexample to Grünbaum's tentative remark in the same paper that none of the other isozonohedra obtainable by indenting it seem to be parallelohedra. A paper is deposited on Zenodo.

Novelty check

The Palomar record and the repository report the author's own search and do not claim the first example for k = 10: solids of the same family, the triacontahedron with four corners indented, were shown on the web as space-fillers without proof, without zone sizes and without reference to Grünbaum's question, by D. Hall (Sketchfab, 2016, the same solid as G. Inchbald's dimpled rhombic triacontahedron) and by J. Kostick (vZome, 2022), whose array shows GET A, a sibling of GET B. GET B itself was not found in the sources DICTO checked. This entry did not repeat that search beyond reading the cited material. Nothing in this registry covered parallelohedra or Grünbaum's zone question.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal for what the Lean states: Palomar records trust level high, with the challenge importing only Mathlib and replay accepted by nanoda and con-ron under Lean 4.35.0-rc2. Unlike a Mathlib-only statement such as a graph's girth, the challenge has to define rhombic monohedral polyhedra, zones and face-to-face tiling itself, in 194 lines; the cube and rhombic-dodecahedron calibrations constrain those definitions but do not replace reading them. Palomar's automated review warned that the metadata claims an independent statement review while listing no reviewer, so who checked the definitions is unclear. As a fact about the literature the result is narrower than the headline: related solids were already known as space-fillers, and what is new is a proof for a specific witness, with zone sizes, tied to Grünbaum's question. Autonomy is ai-assisted: the README says DICTO set the question, found and named the solids and chose the witness, and that a Claude Code agent wrote the definitions, statements, generators and proofs. That puts the discovery with the human and the formal verification with the AI. DICTO is a pseudonym with an ORCID record.
