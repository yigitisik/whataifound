Pointwise convergence of ergodic averages along two commuting transformations

A Lean-checked proof that for two commuting measure-preserving transformations TT and SS of a probability space, the averages N−1∑n<Nf(Tnx)g(Snx)N^{-1}\sum_{n<N} f(T^n x)\, g(S^n x) converge almost everywhere whenever f∈Lpf \in L^p and g∈Lqg \in L^q with 1/p+1/q≤11/p + 1/q \le 1, found by a model asked to adapt OpenAI's recent triangular Hilbert transform result.

What was found

Norm convergence of these bilinear averages has been known since Conze and Lesigne in the 1980s. Pointwise convergence was known when one transformation is a power of the other, by Bourgain's double recurrence theorem, and for distal systems by Donoso and Sun; the general commuting case is the standing pointwise question, and the triangular Hilbert transform is the singular integral long studied as its harmonic-analysis counterpart. According to the repository, GPT-6 Astra Pro was instructed to adapt OpenAI's preprint 'Annular variation of the triangular Hilbert transform at the symmetric point', released on 5 October 2026, solved the problem and wrote a blueprint, and Claude Opus 5.5 then generated the Lean formalization. The statement in Theorems.lean, written by the human authors, uses only Mathlib's definitions of measure-preserving maps, LpL^p membership and limits, and the Palomar record replays the proof against it under the three standard axioms.

Novelty check

Searched arXiv and the web on 2026-10-20 for pointwise convergence of bilinear ergodic averages for commuting transformations. The sources found describe positive answers only for powers of one map and for distal systems. The search also found a second proof of the same theorem, by Guo, Xiao and Zhang (arXiv:2610.26388, posted 19 October 2026), by a different method built on matrix-energy estimates and Gaussian heat flow; its AI-use statement says ChatGPT (GPT-6) helped with the kernel, the proof strategy and one lemma, and it cites the same OpenAI preprint. Neither group refers to the other. This repository's commit history begins on 9 October and it was registered at Palomar on 18 October, so its priority rests on the repository rather than a dated preprint.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal for the stated theorem on the Palomar registration, which replays the proof with two independent kernels against a 33-line Challenge statement built only from Mathlib definitions. That is the anchoring the Dedekind zeta entry treats as enough, and it is why this entry differs from Palomar results whose statements rest on their authors' own definitions. The grade certifies the theorem, not the blueprint or the account of how it was found: the repository says only README.md and Theorems.lean are human-authored, the formalization was generated by a model, and no mathematician is on record as having read the proof. The Palomar abstract names the model as GPT 6 Pro while the repository's AI statement at the registered commit says GPT 6 Astra Pro; the entry follows the repository. Autonomy is ai-led rather than autonomous because the humans chose the problem and supplied the approach, adapting one specific prior result, which is itself an unrefereed preprint from an unreleased model.
