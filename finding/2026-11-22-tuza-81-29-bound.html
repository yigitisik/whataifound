Lean-checked 81/29 bound for Tuza's triangle-cover conjecture

AI research agents built and directed by Tyler B. Hughes proved, and formalized in Lean against a statement that uses only Mathlib, that every finite simple graph GG satisfies τ(G)≤(81/29)ν(G)\tau(G) \le (81/29)\,\nu(G), where ν\nu is the largest number of edge-disjoint triangles and τ\tau the fewest edges meeting every triangle, improving the bound of 165/59165/59 posted by Sichen Wang in September 2026.

What was found

Tuza conjectured in 1981 that τ(G)≤2ν(G)\tau(G) \le 2\nu(G) for every graph, which would be sharp for K4K_4. The general bound stood at Haxell's 66/23≈2.87066/23 \approx 2.870 from 1999 until 2026, when Yi lowered it to 63/2263/22 in August and Wang to 165/59≈2.797165/59 \approx 2.797 on 12 September, with a Lean formalization. The new bound is 81/29≈2.79381/29 \approx 2.793. The Lean project extends Wang's MIT-licensed tuza-lean library with what its abstract calls helper-arborescence coupling, unmerged coloured-family covers and opposite-edge elimination. The challenge file is 101 lines, imports only Mathlib's clique, rational and lattice modules, and defines ν\nu and τ\tau as a supremum over triangle packings and an infimum over triangle transversals; it states the bound in natural-number form (29τ≤81ν29\tau \le 81\nu), in rational form and as the existence of a small transversal, together with the non-vacuity checks ν(K4)=1\nu(K_4) = 1 and τ(K4)=2\tau(K_4) = 2. A 32-vertex companion gadget is checked separately in exact rational Python and is not part of the theorem. The result is one of 41 that mathdiscoveries.org lists, each marked as not yet refereed by a human.

Novelty check

arXiv, 2026-11-23: abs:Tuza AND abs:conjecture returned 84 records, none with a general bound below 165/59165/59; the most recent general improvements are Wang (2609.13831) and Yi (August 2026). Palomar first registered the result on 22 November 2026, and it was on the project's site from 21 November. Within this registry, 2026-10-18-epi-autonomous-research-records includes Tuza's conjecture only for split graphs; no entry covered the general bound. The problem is Erdős Problem 167.

Caveats and known objections

Graded formal because the theorem is machine-checked against a statement that imports only Mathlib, with Palomar's trust level high and its replay accepted by two independent kernels, nanoda and con-ron, under Lean 4.35.0. The statement is short enough to audit by hand, and the K4K_4 checks rule out a vacuous definition of ν\nu or τ\tau. What is not established: no human mathematician has refereed the proof, as the repository's own formalization metadata says, and Palomar's automated review warned that the public account calls the companion gadget independently checked without identifiable evidence for that check; the gadget is outside the formal statement, so the warning bears on the write-up and not on the theorem. Autonomy is ai-led: the metadata says the result was discovered and formalized by AI research agents built and directed by Hughes, which leaves the key insight with the agents, but direction by the person who built them is more than posing the problem, so it is not autonomous. The models behind the agents are not named.
