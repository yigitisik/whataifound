Elsewhere

Four other projects record AI contributions to mathematics. Each one certifies something different, and the differences matter more than they look. This page says what each checks, what none of them check, and where this registry sits.

The landscape

Grouped by what kind of record each one keeps. Descriptions come from the same vocabulary file the finding pages render from, so what is claimed here is what is claimed beside a citation.

Machine-checked elsewhere

Palomar↗

Incubated by the Lean FRO and ICARM, with a scientific advisory board of nine mathematicians.

Snapshots of public Lean repositories, mathematics only.

CertifiesA Lean proof that typechecks against the recorded statement at a pinned commit under a declared axiom set, checked mechanically rather than by human review.

ProofAtlas↗

A platform publishing authored formalizations, in read-only beta.

Lean formalizations and the proof routes behind them, mathematics only.

CertifiesThat a recorded Lean build passed with no unfinished proof steps, under its own evidence contract, which a record can satisfy for checking while still failing for acceptance.

The open problem

MathDB↗

A collaboration between Caltech mathematics faculty, graduate students and undergraduates.

66,736 open problems in research mathematics.

CertifiesNothing mechanically. It records what a problem says, what is known about it, and the standing of any claimed solution.

Also recorded at

vibemathed↗

Community-curated, maintained by Rasmus Lindahl.

Mathematics problems first closed with AI in the loop.

CertifiesNothing mechanically. It is a parallel listing of the same result, carrying its own verification label rather than an independent check.

One result, four different facts

Sendov's conjecture is the clearest example. ProofAtlas hosts Lech Mazur's formalization, which its own record marks Lean checked with a passing build but not accepted, because the retained audit is a summary rather than the full build transcript its evidence contract asks for. Palomar registers Terence Tao's separate formalization, written from his blog digestion rather than from Mazur's proof, and archives its entire dependency tree. MathDB tracks the conjecture itself. vibemathed lists the result.

All four are true at once, and none of them is the whole picture. Priority for the first machine-checked proof belongs to Mazur; the reproducible archived artifact is Tao's; the problem's standing in the literature is MathDB's to state. Our entry for Sendov is the only place a reader sees all four and which one answers which question.

The Jacobian conjecture shows the same thing sharper. MathDB records it as partially solved with the claim not independently verified. We grade the counterexample formally verified. Both are right: they are tracking the conjecture's standing in the literature, which stays open until the literature catches up, and we are grading whether the counterexample checks out, which it does under a symbolic recomputation anyone can rerun in seconds.

What none of them do

Two things on this site have no counterpart anywhere above, and they are the reason it exists.

We grade how much the AI actually did. Every entry carries an autonomy grade on a six-point scale, from a result the model produced with no mathematical input beyond the problem statement, down to one where it retrieved something a human had already proved. It is graded on the strictest defensible reading, so an arguable case takes the weaker grade. No other registry records this, and it is the question most readers are actually asking.

We keep the failures. Refuted results, disputed results, and results that turned out to already exist in the literature stay on the record, marked as such. A registry that publishes only what passes is telling you about its filter, not about the field. One of our entries records a set of Erdős problems an AI was reported to have solved and had in fact located existing solutions to; a registry that only registers successes has nowhere to put that.

We also carry the case against. Every source on an entry is labelled with what it is, and challenge is one of the labels: a rebuttal, a failed replication, or prior work showing the result was already known. Where an entry has no counterargument linked, the review queue says so rather than letting the silence read as consensus.

Beyond mathematics, the comparison stops applying. Every project on this page is mathematics only. Roughly half this registry is biology, medicine, materials, physics, chemistry and the rest.

Corrections welcome

If you maintain one of these projects and this page describes it wrongly, that is a bug and we would rather hear it than keep it. Send a correction, or email us. Descriptions here are generated from one vocabulary file, so a fix lands on this page, on the methodology page and beside every citation at once.

Editorial policy

Announcements enter as claimed, however confident they sound. Nothing is ever deleted: entries are downgraded and annotated, and the history stays public. Every entry carries a novelty check, including when it comes back clean.
